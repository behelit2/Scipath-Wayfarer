Instruction Manual

Explore a procedurally generated post-apocalyptic world, uncover rare remnants of the old civilization, help its settlements, defeat ten regional bosses, and rebuild connections between villages as you liberate their regions.

Campaign objective: defeat the boss in each of the ten regions. Defeating a regional boss unlocks Reconstruction Boards in that region's villages immediately. You can build and guard links during the campaign, then continue rebuilding after all ten bosses fall. Discoveries, confederations, and trade are optional and do not add another boss or a required network size.

Quick Start

  1. Enter a character name and select a difficulty.
  2. Move using the keyboard or by touching the map.
  3. Fight wilderness enemies to earn gold and experience.
  4. Enter villages and speak with their residents.
  5. Check Inventory → Log → Bosses for reported boss coordinates. Turn on a known boss's compass to display a directional arrow near the hero.
  6. Buy equipment, healing items, and sea provisions.
  7. Stay at an inn to heal and register a recovery refuge.
  8. Defeat a regional boss to reduce that region's encounters and unlock its village Reconstruction Boards.
  9. Optionally connect villages in liberated regions and donate equipment to guard their links.
  10. Three or more connected independent villages automatically form a confederation. Separate confederations can establish trade without merging.
  11. Enter unusual landmarks to discover their names and stories. Review them in Log → Discoveries.
  12. Defeat all ten bosses to complete the campaign. Keep exploring, rebuilding, and trading afterward if you wish.

Difficulty

Choose Easy, Normal, or Hard when starting a new game. Difficulty affects starting health and gold, enemy attack strength, rewards, character growth, supply prices, resale values, and defeat penalties. Your selected difficulty is retained in the save.

World and Regional Progression

The world contains grasslands, forests, deserts, frozen reaches, coasts, seas, villages, ruins, abandoned radio towers, rare discoveries, and fixed regional boss lairs. Region level rises with distance from coordinate (0, 0), up to region level 10.

Outer regions contain much stronger enemies. You may enter them early, but doing so can be extremely dangerous.

Each region has its own boss and reconstruction unlock. Defeating the region 1 boss unlocks boards in region 1; defeating the region 2 boss unlocks boards in region 2; the same rule applies through region 10.

Boss order is not enforced. Defeating a boss out of order unlocks its own region, not every lower region. The campaign ending still requires all ten regional bosses to be defeated.

The Log: Bosses, Discoveries, and Trade Missions

Open Inventory → Log to access three tabs. Bosses contains reported lairs, compass controls, and final testimonies. Discoveries records unusual locations you have entered. Trade Missions shows your active delivery and previous mission outcomes.

Undiscovered locations do not reveal their names, coordinates, or descriptions in the Discoveries tab. Their entries remain anonymous until you find them.

Reading any Log tab, changing a boss compass target, or cancelling a trade mission does not consume a battle action. The Network ledger remains a separate record of reconstruction links, patrols, and political membership.

Regional Boss Reports and Compass

Knowledgeable villagers report the coordinates of their region's active boss. These reports are retained in Inventory → Log → Bosses, together with the source of the report, the approximate distance, and the direction from your current position.

Once an undefeated boss's location is known, a Compass button appears within that boss's entry. The button displays its current state: Compass: Off or Compass: On. Click an off button to enable it, or click the active button again to remove the arrow.

Only one boss compass can be active. Turning on another boss's compass automatically switches guidance to that boss; you do not have to turn the previous one off first.

While enabled, a cyan arrow appears approximately two tiles ahead of the hero in the direction of the selected boss. It points toward that boss's reported coordinates and updates as you move. Its placement is based on the boss's direction, not the direction you last moved. The arrow scales when you change tile size.

Near the target, the arrow can sit closer to the hero. At the exact target coordinates, there is no remaining direction to indicate, so the arrow is hidden. Defeating the targeted boss automatically turns its compass off. Unknown and defeated bosses do not offer compass toggles.

Compasses indicate a straight-line direction, not a safe route or an automatically generated path. They do not avoid water, reduce encounters, provide fast travel, or remove boat fees and provision requirements. Plan the journey normally.

The selected boss compass target is retained in your save. Older saves without a compass selection begin with it off. A trade mission has its own red compass and does not replace your cyan boss compass selection.

Rare Discoveries

Thirty hand-authored discoveries are scattered sparsely through the procedural world. Each has an individual tile illustration reflecting the structure or remains found there.

Before you enter a discovery, its map label is Unknown Site. Entering reveals its name and a short account of what you find. That discovery is then retained in Inventory → Log → Discoveries, including its coordinates and text.

These locations do not replace ordinary ruins. Discoveries tell stories; ordinary ruins retain their existing salvage system. Revisiting a discovery lets you read it again without recording a duplicate entry.

Discovery positions are tied to the world seed and remain stable when you save and continue. Finding them is optional and does not unlock or block regional bosses.

Sea Travel and Provisions

Entering water on foot requires a 10-gold boat rental. Landing leaves the boat behind, so a later sea departure requires another rental.

Every water tile consumes one provision. Once provisions reach zero, each additional water tile removes 5% of maximum HP, rounded upward.

Sea starvation can defeat you. Carry enough provisions for the outward journey and the return trip. Reconstruction links, guards, trade agreements, and compasses do not remove boat fees, provision use, or starvation.

Item Shops sell provision packs that cover 200 water tiles. Provisions are added directly to your supply total rather than stored as an inventory item.

Villages, Inns, Ruins, and Radio Towers

Villages contain residents, an inn, and possibly Weapon, Armor, or Item Shops. Not every village has every shop. Knowledgeable residents reveal the coordinates of their region's active boss; those reports are retained in Inventory → Log → Bosses, where you can also enable compass guidance.

A normal inn stay costs 5 + 4 × region level gold. It restores all HP and registers that village as your recovery refuge. A village that is an endpoint of a completed reconstruction link offers free inn stays.

Merely entering a village does not heal you or change your refuge. You must choose an inn stay, even when the stay is free.

Ruins are searched automatically the first time you enter them. They may contain equipment, consumables, or 100–200 sea provisions, but some contain nothing useful. A searched ruin remains permanently explored and does not refill.

Abandoned Radio Towers appear on land at approximately half the frequency of ruins. Their steel mast symbols distinguish them from other landmarks. Enter a tower to turn its transmitter on or off. Activation is optional.

The song button cycles through Song 1, Song 2, and Song 3. Song 1 is the default. Each tower saves its own selection, including while its transmitter is off.

An active transmitter broadcasts its selected song within 75 tiles, measured in a straight line. Leaving the tower does not disable it. Return and choose Turn Transmitter Off to stop that tower's broadcast. Both transmitter states and song selections are retained in your save.

Active towers have a green beacon and blue broadcast markings. Overlapping broadcast areas play one song rather than stacking audio. The nearest active tower determines the song; ties are resolved consistently. Moving into the range of a nearer tower can change what you hear.

Leaving every active broadcast area stops the sound; reentering starts it again. Radio broadcasts are silent while boating, leaving the existing sea-travel music unchanged.

If your browser blocks playback, press a key or tap the game again while within range. Radio broadcasts can be heard during ordinary land travel, village visits, and combat. The audio files Signal.mp3, Signal2.mp3, and Signal3.mp3 belong beside this HTML file. Filenames and capitalization must match exactly. A missing song does not interrupt gameplay.

Combat and Equipment

Attack, use consumables, change equipment, or attempt to flee. Regional boss battles cannot be fled. Mini bosses can be fled, but they have stronger stats and dangerous combat patterns.

Bosses and mini bosses show their next response in the gold telegraph box. Watch for heavy attacks, armor changes, healing, piercing strikes, and multi-strike responses.

  • Critical hits deal 1.75 times normal damage.
  • Armor piercing ignores part of enemy defense.
  • Damage items ignore enemy armor.
  • One ward blocks one complete strike, not an entire multi-strike response.
  • Static Smoke weakens the next strike that would connect; blocking or evading does not consume that weakening.
  • Failed flee attempts trigger the displayed enemy response.
  • Using an item or changing equipment during battle consumes your action.
  • Reading the Log or Network ledger, changing a boss compass, or cancelling a trade mission does not consume an action.

Equipping gear adds its combat benefits while you own and wear it. Selling equipped gear removes those benefits immediately. Donating equipped patrol gear also removes its benefits, but requires a separate confirmation warning.

Experience, Leveling Up, and Sound

Victories award experience. When your XP reaches the requirement shown in the HUD, the hero gains a level. Leveling increases maximum HP according to difficulty, increases base attack, and periodically increases base defense. Your health is fully restored when you level up.

A level-up chime plays whenever an experience award raises the hero's level. If one award grants several levels, the chime plays once rather than layering several copies over one another. Ordinary XP gains and loading a saved game do not play the chime.

Defeat and Regional Victory

Defeat in battle or at sea returns you to your registered refuge with full HP and costs some gold. Inventory, equipment, XP, boss progress, discovered locations, and remaining provisions are retained.

Defeating a regional boss permanently halves random encounter chance in that region, increases your maximum HP, restores health according to difficulty, and records the boss's final words in Inventory → Log → Bosses. If your compass was tracking that boss, it turns off automatically.

That victory also unlocks Reconstruction Boards in every village of the defeated boss's region. You do not need to wait until the campaign ends to begin rebuilding.

Defeat cancels an unfinished relay journey and any active trade mission. The red trade compass disappears, and the failed delivery is recorded in the Trade Missions tab. Completed links, patrols, confederations, and trade agreements remain.

Reconstruction Network - During the Campaign

Visit a Reconstruction Board in a village whose regional boss has been defeated. Start one relay journey at village A, then travel normally to a different village B and establish the link at B's board.

Both endpoint regions must be liberated. The villages may be in the same region or in different unlocked regions. Visiting a village in a locked region does not complete the journey; defeat that region's boss or continue to another eligible village.

The board lists previously visited villages and shows whether their reconstruction is available or locked. Your destination may also be a village you discover along the way.

Only one relay journey can be pending at a time. Starting it costs nothing. You can cancel it without penalty at an available board. A relay journey and a trade mission are separate tasks and can be active at the same time.

Establishing a Basic Link

At the destination, the board previews the construction cost:

  • Gold: 20 + 4 × the higher endpoint region level + ⌈straight-line distance / 10⌉
  • Provisions: 8 + ⌈straight-line distance / 8⌉
  • One consumable selected from your inventory.

The ceiling symbol ⌈ ⌉ means round upward to the next whole number. Construction resources are charged only when you establish the link. Travel expenses, including boat rentals and sea provisions, are additional.

The donated consumable is permanently removed without applying its healing or battle effect. If resources are missing, the journey remains pending while you resupply.

A basic link appears as a gold connection line on the map and makes inns free at both endpoints. A basic link does not reduce encounter chance by itself.

Guarding and Widening a Link

At either endpoint, choose one weapon and one armor, then use the Guard Link button beneath the selection controls. The equipment is permanently donated to establish a patrol.

A guarded link appears as a green connection line. Its protected land corridor halves random encounter chance. Where the regional boss is also defeated, the two reductions multiply, leaving one quarter of the original encounter chance. Overlapping patrol corridors do not stack.

The protected radius around the connection is: 1 + min(2, floor((weapon attack + armor defense) / 14)) tiles. The maximum radius is three tiles.

Better gear can widen an existing patrol using Widen Patrol. Equal or narrower replacements are refused, and previously donated equipment is not returned.

Donating equipped gear requires confirmation and lowers your attack and/or defense when the gear is removed. Patrols protect land within their corridors only; they do not prevent fixed boss battles, create roads, provide fast travel, or make water safe. Ordinary travel risks still apply.

Tracking Your Work

After your first regional boss victory, open Inventory → Network to view established links, guarded links, towns connected, the largest connected network, confederations, trade relationships, and any pending relay journey.

Physical connectivity and political membership are different. The largest connected network can contain several distinct confederations joined by inter-confederation links.

Reconstruction adds no extra bosses or mandatory network quota. Existing links and patrols remain after campaign completion, and rebuilding can continue indefinitely.

Confederations

When a connected group contains three or more independent villages, it automatically forms a confederation. The villages need to belong to one connected group; every village does not need a direct link to every other member.

A confederation receives a name based on its founding settlements. Its identity and membership are saved, and residents acknowledge their new political association.

Smaller independent groups linked to one existing confederation can join it. Independent settlements connected to multiple confederations do not automatically force those political entities together.

Established confederations never merge because of a reconstruction link. A link between their cities connects distinct political entities and can support trade between them.

The Reconstruction Board identifies a village's membership. The Network ledger lists confederations separately from the physical connection network.

Trade Agreements and Delivery Missions

Once separate confederations exist, a completed reconstruction link between their cities can support a trade agreement. Visit a member village's Reconstruction Board and use the Confederation Trade area to establish an available agreement.

Establishing the agreement has no additional construction charge. Building the inter-confederation link still uses the normal reconstruction costs, and journeys still have ordinary travel expenses.

Each confederation has a distinctive export associated with one of its founding cities. Delivery offers name that resource, the destination city in another confederation, and the gold reward before you accept.

Accepting a mission supplies a sealed consignment. Its cargo is tracked with the mission rather than mixed with equipment and consumables, so it cannot be sold, used, or donated accidentally. No separate gathering or crafting step is required.

You may have one active trade mission at a time. Its red compass points toward the specified destination city. It can appear alongside the cyan boss compass without changing your selected boss target.

Entering the exact destination city completes the delivery automatically. You receive the advertised gold reward once, the mission is recorded as completed, and the red compass disappears.

Cancel an active delivery through Inventory → Log → Trade Missions or an available Reconstruction Board. Cancellation gives no reward and removes the red compass. Defeat also ends the mission, recording a failed delivery.

Trade agreements do not provide safe passage. The red compass shows direction only, and mission cargo does not replace sea provisions. Carry supplies and prepare for the region levels along your route.

Active missions survive saving and continuing. Completed, cancelled, and failed missions are retained in the Trade Missions tab. Villagers discuss their confederation's export and established trade partners.

Saving and Continuing

The game saves automatically after progress changes. Use Save to save immediately, then choose Continue Saved Game on the start screen when you return.

Boss compass selection, discoveries, reconstruction links, patrols, confederation membership, trade agreements, mission records, transmitter states, and each tower's selected song are retained with your other progress.

Existing compatible saves begin with no discoveries recorded, Song 1 selected at each tower, and no active trade mission. Eligible existing reconstruction networks can form confederations when the updated game loads them.

Saves are stored in this browser. Starting a new game replaces the existing save after confirmation; Delete Save removes it. Clearing browser storage or opening the game in another browser can make the saved game unavailable.
